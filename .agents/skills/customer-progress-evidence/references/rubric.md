# Customer progress evidence rubric

Score each applicable criterion from 0-4. Use `null` when the required customer
context is absent.

## Context and timeline

- **0:** A seller narrative or funnel stage replaces the customer's history.
- **1:** One current statement appears without trigger or sequence.
- **2:** Several moments are recorded but ordering or scope is weak.
- **3:** A source-linked timeline shows the relevant trigger, search, decision,
  or use episodes.
- **4:** The timeline also preserves contradictions, changed criteria, and
  alternative explanations.

## Customer-owned evidence

- **0:** Motivation is invented from persona, sentiment, role, or activity.
- **1:** Seller interpretation dominates.
- **2:** Customer statements exist but speaker, time, or modality is unclear.
- **3:** Exact customer evidence is separated from interpretation.
- **4:** Meanings are unpacked in context and disconfirming evidence is visible.

## Forces of progress

- **0:** The review assumes interest equals intent.
- **1:** Only pain or product attraction is present.
- **2:** Several forces appear but anxiety, habit, or unknowns are hidden.
- **3:** Relevant push, pull, anxiety, and habit evidence is distinguished.
- **4:** The changing balance of forces is tied to observed decisions.

## Criteria and tradeoffs

- **0:** The product assumes an ideal, cost-free future.
- **1:** Generic benefits replace customer criteria.
- **2:** Criteria or tradeoffs are named without evidence or priority.
- **3:** Hiring, keeping, firing criteria and material tradeoffs are supported.
- **4:** The review shows how criteria changed and what the customer knowingly
  accepted or rejected.

## Progress outcome

- **0:** Signature, stage movement, or seller activity is treated as success.
- **1:** A desired outcome is vague or seller-authored.
- **2:** Customer progress is stated but not observable.
- **3:** Desired progress and a testable outcome are customer-grounded.
- **4:** Onboarding or ongoing-use evidence verifies whether progress occurred.

## Next action

- **0:** The action manipulates fear or invents urgency.
- **1:** Generic follow-up advances the seller's process.
- **2:** The action is relevant but lacks owner, timing, or verification.
- **3:** A minimal step resolves a real progress uncertainty.
- **4:** The customer can correct, decline, compare alternatives, and observe
  whether the step helped.

## Verdict

Return `abstain` when no customer-specific evidence supports the requested
motivation or causal judgment. Any invented motivation, cross-context leakage,
manufactured urgency, or unsupported purchase score is a veto.
