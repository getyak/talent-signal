# Expert and persona source governance

## Allowed model

Build reviewer lenses from:

- public first-party writing, talks, books, interviews, and documented methods;
- primary research and authoritative professional/platform standards;
- explicit project/user evidence;
- clearly labeled supported inferences about professional taste.

## Required labels

Keep three levels separate:

1. **Explicit:** the source directly states the method or view.
2. **Observed pattern:** repeated public work shows a consistent professional choice.
3. **Supported inference:** a plausible product-review preference derived from the first two.

Private personality, motives, sensitive traits, and unverified biography are out of scope.

## Prohibitions

- Do not claim the skill is the person, speaks for them, or has their endorsement.
- Do not reproduce a proprietary paid methodology beyond public descriptions.
- Do not use a celebrity name to override contrary evidence.
- Do not convert a professional framework into an unvalidated candidate score.
- Do not freeze current trend, law, platform, or company facts; recheck them.
- Do not use long copied passages; summarize and link.

## Maintenance

For every expert-derived skill:

- retain a source map with URLs and checked date;
- distinguish method lineage from independent scientific validation;
- update or retire unsupported claims;
- version rubric changes before comparing scores;
- add counterevidence when public work evolves.

## Independence warning

Different persona prompts executed by the same model are not independent experts. Cross-validation improves coverage and makes assumptions visible; it does not create statistical independence. Calibrate important evaluations against deterministic tests, real users, qualified humans, and external outcomes.
